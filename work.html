01 · UniConnect · Tzu Chi University

One marketplace for everything a campus passes around.

Marketplace Lost & found Donations Bilingual UI AI assistant

The problem

Everything a student wanted to sell, give away or recover was scattered across group chats that emptied out every semester. A textbook worth passing on was easier to bin than to advertise, and a lost wallet depended on whoever happened to still be in the right chat.

What we built

One platform doing four jobs — buy, sell, donate, and report lost items. Sign-in is limited to @gms.tcu.edu.tw addresses, so every listing belongs to a real person on campus, while anyone can browse without an account. Listings filter by category, condition and pickup spot, and the whole interface runs in English and 繁體中文 because the campus does.

What changed

Reuse stopped depending on being in the right group chat. Listings persist past the semester, lost property has one place to be posted, and the donation route means usable things leave campus instead of the bin.

02 · Representative trade workflow · Trade & fit-out

The quote that used to eat every Friday night.

Website Google Ads Quote automation Scoped delivery

The problem

Meridian had no website and quoted every job by text message from the van. Enquiries arrived with no measurements, so each one cost two phone calls before it could be priced. Good jobs went cold while the owner was on site, and there was no way to tell which of them had come from the Facebook page he was paying to boost.

What we built

A five-page site leading with the two services carrying the best margin, a small Google Ads budget aimed only at fitted-furniture searches in three postcodes, and a quote form that asks for the measurements up front and prices the standard jobs itself. Anything unusual still routes to a human — it just arrives already half-answered.

What changed

Enquiries now land with the dimensions already filled in, so a quote goes out the same afternoon instead of the following weekend. Ad spend became traceable to individual jobs, which meant one of the two campaigns could be switched off without guessing.

03 · Representative retail workflow · Local retail

Phone orders off the notepad, and nothing lost again.

Website Order automation AI enquiry sorting Rapid turnaround

The problem

A small grocer taking weekly veg-box orders by phone, written on a pad by the till. Orders were missed on busy mornings, the same three questions about delivery days were answered fifty times a week, and nobody could say which customers had quietly stopped ordering.

What we built

A one-page site with the week's boxes and a standing-order form, so regulars set it once and stop phoning. An automation confirms each order, sends a reminder the night before the cut-off, and flags anyone who hasn't ordered in three weeks. An assistant on the site answers the delivery-day questions in the shop's own wording.

What changed

Mornings stopped being interrupted by the phone, and a missed order became something the system notices rather than something a customer has to complain about. Lapsed regulars are now a short list to call instead of a thing discovered by accident.
